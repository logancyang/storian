01

The problem

A creator comes home from a trip with 70 GB of S-Log3 4K H.265: 352 clips, 1.3 hours, almost no dialogue. Turning that into a 15-minute cinematic film in the style of Life of Riza or Natalie Lynn takes days of logging before a single creative decision is made.

  • Volume. Hours of footage at ~100 Mbps 10-bit 4:2:2. Uploading it to a cloud tool is slow and expensive, and decoding all of it is slow even locally.
  • Log footage. Flat, desaturated S-Log3 frames fool both humans and vision models. Analysis must see a display-referred image.
  • No transcript to lean on. The story lives in pictures: shot sizes, motion, light, faces. Speech-to-text gives you nothing.
  • Taste is the product. A sequence that "uses the good shots" is not a film. Perspective changes, cutting on action, pacing curves, VO gaps, motif and bookends are what make it feel directed.
  • Iteration. The human wants to say "make act 3 faster" or "swap this for something with more motion", not to drag clips around.

03

Gaps in the market

  • Transcript-centric story logic. Eddie, Quickture, Descript, Gling, Resolve IntelliScript and video-use all build the spine from speech. Wordless B-roll is treated as cutaways, never as the narrative.
  • No shot grammar. No product treats shot size, lens feel, camera movement, screen direction or match-on-action as editing variables. ShotBench (NeurIPS 2025) measured frontier VLMs below 60% on these dimensions, so the gap is real and needs explicit structure.
  • Craft rules aren't modeled. Wide→close progressions, focal-length contrast, cuts on motion, J/L cuts, per-act pacing curves, VO gaps, motif and bookends: none of this is encoded anywhere.
  • Style learning is shallow. Selects, invideo and editstyle copy pacing, captions and effects. Nobody extracts shot-grammar statistics from a reference film, such as size-transition matrices, ASL per section, movement ratio or VO share.
  • Log footage at scale. No tool claims log-aware analysis, and cloud tools bill per hour or per GB. A local-first pipeline with LUT-corrected proxies and keyframe-only decode wins on both cost and privacy.
  • MCP is everywhere; inspectable artifacts aren't. Agent access alone no longer differentiates. An open, file-based pipeline that an agent can read, diff and revise in conversation does.
  • No storyboard of real footage. Storyboard tools are pre-production image generators. Nothing produces a post-production paper edit of real frames with in/out timecodes, labels and a playable animatic.
04

The design

Storian is a small, deterministic CLI plus a written body of director knowledge. The CLI does everything cheap and mechanical. The agent does everything that needs eyes or taste. The human only talks.

Principle

Agent-first, not agent-added

Commands are idempotent, print compact JSON, and say what to do next (storian status). The README is written for agents; AGENTS.md is the operating manual.

Principle

Files are the API

clips.json, labels/*.json, story.json and sequence.json. Nothing lives in a database the agent can't open, and every human request is a surgical edit to a file.

Principle

Local-first, keyframe-only

Raw footage never leaves the disk and is never fully decoded. Keyframes (≈1/s) decode with hardware acceleration in under a second per clip, whatever the bitrate.

Principle

Log-aware from frame one

Camera gamma is read from metadata, and S-Log3 frames go through a generated S-Gamut3.Cine→709 LUT before any model or human sees them.

Principle

Taste is written down

knowledge/director.md is an opinionated playbook covering structure, pacing, sequence patterns, cut types and VO rules. Rules can be broken only on purpose, with a written reason.

Principle

Review where editors review

A localhost storyboard: theme, mood and idea first, then acts of shot cards with frames, labels, in/out, VO lines and director notes, plus a playable animatic. It updates live as the agent edits.

Architecture

05

Encoding the director

The playbook (storian/knowledge/director.md) is what makes storian a director rather than a search engine. It is plain prose and tables, so any model can follow it and any human can argue with it.

Story before footage

  • A controlling idea in one sentence: what the film is about, not what it shows.
  • A visual motif that opens, returns and pays off. That's how a plotless film feels designed.
  • An arc built on change (outsider → insider, noise → quiet), ordered by the arc, not the camera clock.
  • No script? Three treatments with distinct moods, cast only from footage that exists.

Cutting

  • Change size by two steps or the focal feel on every cut; wide ↔ tele on one subject is the cheapest cinematic trick.
  • Motivated cuts first: match-on-action, cut-on-motion, graphic match, whip, J/L. Each shot records how it enters.
  • Keep screen direction within a scene; reset with a neutral or insert shot.
  • Pause VO for hero shots, sound moments, chapter starts and the turn.

Learning from reference films (v0.2)

The user drops one or more films they love into refs/. Storian detects cuts (ffmpeg scdet / PySceneDetect), extracts a mid-frame per shot, and has the agent label every shot with the same grammar as the footage. The result is style.json: ASL by section, a shot-size transition matrix, focal and movement ratios, cut-type frequencies, VO share and act proportions. That profile overrides the playbook's defaults, and storian check lints the sequence against it ("your act 2 ASL is 4.1 s; the reference averages 2.8 s"). Selects copies an edit's pacing; storian copies its grammar.

08

Roadmap

v0.1 · now

Storyboard MVP

Scan, log-aware frames, agent labeling, digest, treatments, sequence, craft lints, previews, live storyboard + animatic.

v0.2

Learn from reference films

storian learn refs/*.mp4 → style.json (shot grammar statistics) that overrides the defaults and drives check.

v0.3

Timeline handoff

OpenTimelineIO as the internal model; export FCPXML / .otio that Resolve imports directly, with markers carrying the VO lines and director notes. Optional davinci-resolve-mcp for Studio users to build the timeline live.

v0.4

Cheaper eyes

Pluggable labelers: the agent itself (default), a local VLM (Qwen-VL / ShotVL) for first-pass labels that the agent then reviews, or Gemini / Twelve Labs for people who prefer cloud. Embedding search for match-cut candidates.

v0.5

Sound & VO

Music beat detection to snap montage cuts, nat-sound peak detection (tram bells, sizzle), and VO timing from a recorded scratch track, so VO lines land on real durations.

later

MCP server & multi-folder projects

The same commands exposed as MCP tools for any agent host, and whole trips spanning many folders and cameras with cross-camera colour matching.

Risks

  • Label quality bounds the film. VLMs are weak on shot grammar. Mitigated by contact sheets with timestamps, motion signals from pixels, and a written schema, and later by specialist models and reference calibration.
  • Token cost at 10+ hours. Contact sheets keep it linear (≈2.5k tokens per clip). Local first-pass labeling is the plan for very large libraries.
  • Taste is subjective. That's why taste is a readable file, reference films can override it, and every shot carries a "why" the human can veto.
